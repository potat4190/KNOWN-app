The roast

The sharpest judge's line will be: "It asks me how I feel, then ignores my answer." Here's everything a sharp judge is likely to poke at, worst first.

  1. Personalization you can't feel.

    • In Paper, all five "sad" statements lead to the same screen, and that screen then asks the question again.

    • In Lamplight, "What stays with you?" changes nothing.

    • In Companion, the answer on S10 ("Does this connect?") is never used.

    • A judge who taps two different answers and gets the same thing will call it fake.

  2. Too many doors before the verse.

    • In Paper and Companion, the path runs Mode → Pictures → Closer → Connection → Meet → Context → Words.

    • "Meet David in this moment" shows no verse at all, so it's a promise with nothing behind it.

    • Three screens of setup before Scripture feel like a lecture.

  3. Features that pretend.

    • The language picker does nothing in Canvas or Companion.

    • Reminders are saved but never sent, in Paper and Lamplight.

    • The "Link for your guide" points back to the prototype itself.

    • Judges press every button. A fake button costs more than a missing one.

  4. Promises the code breaks.

    • Lamplight says "Stays on this phone. No one else can see it." But message text goes out for AI translation, and feedback is stored with a user ID.

    • Paper and Canvas say "Only the items you check will be saved," yet Paper pre-checks "The message I wrote" even after you tap "Not today." Canvas's "Keep this for later" saves everything with no checklist.

    • For a Myanmar student, a privacy claim that isn't true is a safety issue. It's not a nitpick.

  5. The safety gap.

    • Companion has no crisis help on the student's side. It shows Psalm 13:3, a line about sleeping in death, with no safety net, and Hannah's safety copy is still a placeholder.

    • The other three versions lead with 988, the US crisis line, in an app for people from other countries.

  6. Anger and fear get brushed off.

    • In Paper, picking anger or fear on its own sends you to Psalm 139 with "Thank you for sharing what feels close."

    • Lament research says complaint to God is part of healthy faith (see Research). Canvas and Lamplight handle this well, so steal their passages.

  7. Scripture versions don't match.

    • Cards say KJV or WEBBE, but "Open in YouVersion" opens the NIV.

    • KJV wording like "As the hart panteth" is a wall for an English learner.

    • Companion shows "NIV?" on screen.

  8. Dead ends and loops.

    • In Companion, Pray, Sit and Reflect have no way to finish. You can only finish by sending a message.

    • In Paper and Canvas, tapping Back after saving lets you save the same moment twice.

  9. Copy slips judges will read aloud.

    • "Hear The writer's words" and "the writer of Lamentations's" (wrong capital letters and doubled possessives).

    • "Thank you for coming tonight," said at 2 p.m.

    • Lamplight's "With someone I trust" screen says the person is "beside you" and then "They won't see anything."

  10. "Where's the AI?" Matching is a hand-written lookup table, and AI only translates outgoing messages. That's defensible and even a strength (see Q&A), but you need to say so out loud before they ask.

The recommended flow

There are six core screens: one tap reaches the first picture, four reach a verse, and seven finish. There's a two-tap fast lane for someone with no words, and there are no dead ends.

Today, finishing takes 10 taps in Lamplight, at least 10 in Canvas, 12 in Paper and about 16 in Companion. In this flow, picking a statement is the only step that chooses the passage. Showing that statement back to the user on the Scripture screen proves it mattered.

Friction reducers

The biggest win is showing the user's own words back to them on the Scripture screen. For example: You said: "I smile in class, but inside I feel very far away." One line proves the choice mattered, and it costs nothing to build. Below are the other friction reducers, grouped by what each one fixes.

Fewer taps before the user gets something

  • Scripture by tap 4: Begin, a picture, Next, a statement. Ask for language once at first launch, and move the "on my own / with someone" choice out of the main path.

  • One primary button per screen. Everything else is a text link. Paper has this backwards on the sit, pray and write screens, where "Finish for now" is the primary button.

  • Progress dots ("Step 2 of 4") so the user knows how much is left.

Less language load

  • Use the user's first language everywhere: the interface, a small label under each picture, and the verse.

  • Use one plain, modern translation for English instead of KJV. Check which ones you're licensed to show through YouVersion.

  • Pre-fill everything (prayer, message) so users edit rather than write from scratch. That's easier in a second language, and blank-page writing has weak evidence (see Research).

  • Use time-neutral copy: "today" or "right now" instead of "tonight."

Trust and safety

  • Write one privacy line that's true. For example: "Your prayers stay on this phone. If you translate a message, that text is sent to a translation service."

  • Add a quick exit and "clear everything." Don't show past moment titles on the home screen, because a phone may be shared.

  • Ask before throwing away a prayer the user typed.

  • Put Help in the header on every screen, set to the user's country.

No dead ends

  • Every screen gets one way forward and one way to finish.

  • Saving happens once. Back after saving should not let the user save the same moment again.

  • The swap gives a real alternative passage, and falls back to Psalm 139 after two swaps.

Legibility (Lamplight already does all of this, so keep it)

  • Atkinson Hyperlegible at an 18px base

  • Tap targets of 44px or more

  • Visible focus rings

  • Reduced-motion support

Claims to avoid

  • "Journaling helps." The overall effect is small (r = .075, Frattaroli 2006). In 11 trials with Asian participants it was close to zero (g = 0.05, Guo 2023). Say "optional reflection" instead.

  • "Clinically proven" or "reduces depression." KNOWN itself hasn't been tested. Say "grounded in research, piloting with students."

  • "Completely private." Don't say it until translation and feedback match the claim.

  • The "50% higher survival from social ties" statistic. It's widely quoted, but we didn't verify it against Holt-Lunstad 2010. Cite the paper without the number.

  • Crisis numbers you haven't checked. 9% of suicide-prevention apps listed a wrong number. Martinengo et al. 2019
